# CNN Model Activity

The article by Wall (2019) was read which discusses the potential bias and ethical dilemmas concerning the use of facial recognition by the authorities. One key issue is privacy where individuals will be monitored without their consent. This CNN technology has been proven to be biased against darker skin individuals, especially for women. This comes as no surprise when only a few years ago, computers had difficulty distinguishing between muffins and chihuahas. Predictive policing comes at high stakes where innocent people may be targeted, especially those that are darker skinned.

A potential positive use might be FR technology in prisons to keep track of prisoners, especially those which are violent. In this case, it could improve safety and leave less room for error.

A worrying deployment would be FR use in the military. Where we already have semi-autonomous deployment of multiple weapons, why should we make these fully autonomous using a technology which is known to be faulty and biased and which might target innocent civilians, leading to potential war crimes and mass destruction? This begs the question of who would actually be held responsible in the case of such deployment.

Wall (2019) alludes to the fact that AI should not be privately managed but rather a multi-disciplinary stakeholder approach is needed to ensure ethical use and proper regulation.
